I have given the pilau at St. Cyprian's Annual Harvest Fundraiser every year from 1981 until 1989, which was the last year my health permitted me to make the full preparation on my own. I made the pilau in the same pot each time. The pot — the sufuria — was given to me by my mother. It is aluminium, with riveted handles, and it holds approximately twelve litres when full. I have cooked in it many hundreds of times. I know its weight when it is full and when it is half-full and when it is nearly empty. I know how it sits on a surface. I know when a surface is not level.

On the morning of 14 August 1987, I arrived at the church hall at 9:00 in the morning to begin the final stage of the preparations. The serving table was already in its place along the eastern wall, beneath the second window. I placed the sufuria at the centre of the table as I always did. When I placed it, I noticed that the table was not entirely level. The sufuria did not settle in the way it settles on a surface that is flat. It settled with a very slight shift toward the side where the raffle stand had been placed. Not a large shift. But I noticed it.

I said something to someone about this. I cannot now recall who I said it to. I believe it was one of the members of the organising committee who was present in the hall at that time, because they were the ones moving about and attending to the preparations. I said, as I recall, that the table was not sitting quite right and that something should be looked at. I was not told what, if anything, was done in response. I placed the sufuria and continued with my own preparations in the kitchen area adjacent to the hall. I assumed that if something was to be done, it would be done.

When the sufuria slid, it slid in the direction I had observed the tilt. I was not standing at the table at that moment. I had stepped away to attend to something in the kitchen. When I heard the sound, I came back into the hall immediately. The sufuria was on the floor and the raffle stand was on the floor and the tickets were on the floor and Father Kamau had stopped speaking. The sufuria was not damaged. I confirmed this before anything else. I took it home with me after the event was over, as I always did. It is still in my possession. The Heritage Trust has seen it.

No one has asked me about the table since 1987. I was not asked in 1988, or in 1991 when the Trust was formed, or in 1993 when the other testimonies were collected. The Heritage Trust archivist wrote to me in 2023 asking whether I would be willing to give an account, and I agreed, and this is that account. I am eighty-four years old. I do not know why no one asked me before now, and I have decided not to spend further time wondering about it. I know what I observed on the morning of 14 August 1987. The table was not level. I said so to someone whose name I cannot recall. That is what I have to say. I do not believe there is anything else.
