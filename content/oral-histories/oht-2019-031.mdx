I have been a member of the Umoja Estate Residents' Association since I moved to the estate in 1996. I joined the Association at the first opportunity available to me after moving in, because I consider participation in one's residential association to be a civic responsibility of the same order as any other. I have attended every Annual General Meeting of the Association since 1996. I am aware that this is considered notable by some members and that it has been remarked upon. I do not consider it notable. I consider it a straightforward matter of obligation.

On the evening of 17 April 2001, I arrived at the Estate Meeting Hall at approximately 6:45 in the evening, which was fifteen minutes before the doors opened and forty-five minutes before the scheduled commencement. I arrived at this time because it is my habit to arrive before the stated time for any formal meeting. Chair 14 was not in the hall when I arrived. I had sat in Chair 14 at every meeting of the Association since 1996. I am not a person who requires a particular chair. However, Chair 14 was where I sat, and it was not there, and when I examined the room I found that there were no additional chairs available beyond those already placed.

I stood for the meeting. I stood at the position where Chair 14 would have been, against the left wall toward the back of the arrangement. The meeting lasted two hours and four minutes, which I know because I noted the time of adjournment in my pocket diary as I customarily do. I did not find the act of standing for two hours and four minutes particularly difficult, though I will acknowledge that the floor at that location is concrete. I did not raise the matter of the missing chair during the meeting because the meeting had an agenda and a motion before it, and I did not consider it appropriate to introduce a personal observation about furniture into proceedings that were concerned with the maintenance levy.

No other attendee offered me their chair. I did not expect any other attendee to offer me their chair. The other attendees were engaged with the agenda, as was I. I was paying full attention to the proceedings. I was not able to vote on the maintenance levy motion because signing the attendance register required access to the table at the front of the room, and I was not in a position from which I could reach that table without disrupting the row of attendees between myself and it. I did not disrupt them. I note that the motion was carried by seven votes to three.

I have brought my own folding chair to every Annual General Meeting of the Association since April 2002. I stand at meetings when required. I have always stood when required. I do not think this requires elaboration. I have not been asked by any member of the Association, the committee, or any other party about the events of 17 April 2001. If I were asked, I would say what I have said here: Chair 14 was not present. I stood. I do not know where the chair went. I have not found it necessary to know where the chair went. I brought my own.
