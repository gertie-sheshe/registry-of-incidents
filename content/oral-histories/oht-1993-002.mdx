I had been the priest at St. Cyprian's since 1974. The Annual Harvest Fundraiser was one of the events I presided over with the most care, because it was an event that mattered to the congregation in a practical and material way, and I have always believed that a priest must attend to material matters with the same seriousness he brings to spiritual ones. Mama Edna Mutua's pilau was, by the time of the 1987 fundraiser, as much a part of the occasion as the opening hymn. I do not say this to diminish the hymn.

On the morning of 14 August 1987, I arrived at the church hall at 8:00 to supervise the final preparations. The serving table was already in its customary position along the eastern wall. The raffle stand had been placed beside it. I spoke with two members of the organising committee that morning and they assured me that everything was in order. I believe I noticed, in passing, that the legs of the raffle stand did not appear to be sitting evenly on the floor — one leg may have been shorter than the others, or the floor may have been uneven at that point. I did not act on this observation. I mention it not to assign blame for what followed but because it is part of what I remember, and I was asked to give what I remember.

I was addressing the congregation on the subject of the prizes when the sufuria moved. I did not see it move. I heard the impact first — a sound I would describe as sudden and definitive — and when I looked toward the serving table the stand was already on the ground. The tickets were in the air. Mama Edna was at the table. A great many people rose from their seats, which was understandable but not, in my assessment, helpful to the immediate situation. I asked for calm. I received something approaching it.

I called the pause because I felt it was necessary. There followed a period of approximately twenty minutes during which the collected members of the congregation and the organising committee attempted to assess whether a fair draw could be conducted from the recovered tickets. The discussion was earnest and extended. I listened to it. I concluded that a draw under those circumstances would not be accepted as fair by all parties, and that proceeding would create grievances which would outlast any prize. I announced that the raffle would be concluded at a future date and I continued with the remainder of the programme. I did not call a second pause because I did not believe a second pause would resolve anything that the first had not.

I left St. Cyprian's in 1995. I am informed that the raffle was never subsequently held and that the prize was never awarded. I regret this. The Njoroge family donated that prize in good faith and it was received in good faith and it was then lost in circumstances that no one has fully accounted for. I called the pause because I felt it was necessary. I did not feel it was necessary to call another one after that. The Lord provides clarity in His own time. I have not always found this to be a sufficient answer when applied to the matter of a two-night stay at Mombasa Palms Guesthouse that was never awarded to anyone.
