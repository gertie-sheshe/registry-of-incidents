I was seated at the front table — the table positioned nearest the serving area, against the east wall. I had a clear and unobstructed view of the serving table throughout the afternoon. I am aware that some people have found this significant. I was at the front table because I arrived at the event in good time and there was a seat available there. I have attended the St. Cyprian's Harvest Fundraiser since 1979 and I have sat near the front at the majority of them. I am not a person who arrives late to these occasions.

The serving table was perfectly stable. I was watching it with a degree of attention that I would not ordinarily bring to a serving table, because I had decided that I would take a second serving of the pilau before the raffle began, and I was observing the level of the sufuria in relation to the servings already given in order to assess whether a second serving would be feasible before the level became too low to serve from adequately. I was not watching the table because I was concerned about its structural integrity. I had no reason to be concerned about its structural integrity. It appeared to be a normal table.

What happened, happened quickly. I can say that I observed the sufuria move. I cannot say with certainty what caused it to move. I can say with certainty that no person was in contact with the serving table at the moment the sufuria moved. I was watching it. The movement appeared to originate from within the table surface — a shift, or a slight vibration — and the sufuria then reached the edge and fell. I did not cause this. I was seated approximately ninety centimetres from the table, at a separate table, and I was not holding anything.

I stood up because the stand had fallen and there were tickets on the floor and it seemed appropriate to assist in recovering them. I collected tickets for some minutes. I do not recall exactly how many. I have since been informed, by means that I consider indirect and not entirely fair, that the manner in which I stood has been described as unusual by at least one other witness. I am not able to account for how my standing appeared to others. I can only account for why I stood, which I have done.

I understand that my testimony is described in the Heritage Trust's records as disputed. I accept this designation without agreeing with its implication. I cannot prove that the table was stable in any documentary or measurable sense. I can only say that I was watching it, that it appeared stable, and that I have given this account consistently since 1993 without alteration. I was not responsible for the serving table, for the raffle stand, or for the raffle. I was a member of the congregation who attended the fundraiser as I had done every year since 1979. What happened, happened. I was not responsible for the stand.
